RosterView — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 October 2026

The short version. Your roster is stored on your phone. Your airline password is never seen by RosterView. RosterView needs an account, because its point is comparing days off with your crew — but which other pilots can see your roster is your choice: Open for any RosterView pilot, or Private for only the pilots you accept. Your logbook, if you use it, is visible to you alone.

Your airline login

RosterView has no airline built into it. You tell it the web address of the page where you read your roster (Profile & settings → My airline's roster page), and the app opens that page so you can sign in there yourself. Your airline username, password and any image or code verification are handled entirely by that website. RosterView does not read, record, store or transmit them, and they are never sent to our servers.

If you choose to save your airline sign-in details so the app can fill the form for you, they are stored in the iPhone's own Keychain on your device. They are never uploaded, and RosterView never submits the form for you — you always press Login yourself.

Your roster

When you import, your roster is read from the page and stored on your device. A copy of the original roster page is also saved on your device, under "Original", so you can check the app's times against the source.

If you are signed in, each roster page you import is also kept in your account: the page, a picture of it, and the duties the app read from it. This is so you can get your "Original" pages back on a new phone, and so that when the app misreads an airline's page the developer can see exactly what went wrong and fix it. Other pilots never see these pages, whatever your sharing setting. You can delete every page held for you from the app (Profile & settings → Delete the pages held for me). Pages from imports after that are kept again, for the same reasons; deleting your account removes them all.

Sharing with other pilots

RosterView requires an account. When you create one, your roster is shared with other RosterView pilots by default, so you can compare days off and find swaps. You can change that whenever you like in Profile & settings, and your visibility setting decides which other pilots can see your roster:

  • Private — your roster is stored on our server and can only be read by pilots whose request you have accepted. Other pilots see that your roster is private and can send you a request; nothing more of it.
  • Open (the default when you create an account) — your roster can be read by any signed-in RosterView pilot.

Accounts that chose the earlier setting "Private — no other pilots" keep it until they pick one of these: no other pilot can read their roster, and it is not uploaded for sharing.

These rules are enforced by the database itself, not merely by the app. The developer can read rosters and imported pages in order to fix import problems, and does not share them.

Your logbook

The logbook is stored on your device. While you are signed in, a copy is also kept in your account a few seconds after each change, so that losing or replacing your phone does not cost you your logbook. It holds what you put in it: flights, aircraft, times, the names of the crew you flew with, and any signatures collected in the app. Other pilots can never see it, and it is not shared with anyone. You can also save a copy to a file yourself (Logbook → settings → Save a copy).

On iPhone, when you are on Wi-Fi, a copy is also written to your own iCloud Drive, in a folder called RosterView that you can see in the Files app. Those files are yours: they are stored by Apple under your Apple Account, we cannot see them, and they stay until you delete them in Files — deleting the app or your RosterView account does not remove them.

Logbook files you send us

If you email us an export from another logbook app so that we can bring your flights into RosterView for you, we use that file only for that import. We do not share it, and we delete the file and the email once your import is done, and in any case within 30 days.

Weather

To show the weather for a layover, the app asks Open-Meteo for the forecast at that airport's location. Only the airport's coordinates and the date are sent; nothing about you is.

Other people's names

Airline rosters list the crew you are flying with. Those names are stored on your device as part of your roster, and are included if you choose to share your roster. Please treat colleagues' details with the same care you would the printed roster.

Face ID

You can require Face ID before RosterView will open. The check is performed by iOS on your device; RosterView is told only whether it succeeded, and never receives your face data or your passcode.

What we do not do

  • No analytics, tracking or advertising.
  • No third-party trackers or advertising SDKs, and no selling or sharing of data with anyone.
  • No location collection.
  • No access to your contacts. A photo is read only if you pick one.
  • Your calendar is written to only if you ask the app to add your roster to it, and only in a calendar of its own called RosterView. Your other calendars are never read or changed.

Deleting your data

  • Account and everything in it: in the app, tap your photo → Profile & settings → Delete my account. This permanently removes your account, any rosters you shared, the roster pages held for you, your logbook backup and your connections with other pilots.
  • Everything on the phone: delete the app.
  • Logbook copies in your iCloud Drive: delete the RosterView folder in the Files app.

Children

RosterView is intended for professional airline crew and is not directed at children.
